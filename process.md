# From zero to launch — Akibit

This process incorporates the user's Websitecreationfromzerotolaunch.docx. Its working rule is adopted: reduce scope before reducing reliability, and ship the smallest complete website that achieves the business goal.

## One-page brief

The visitor is a small-business owner or founder who needs a website, a conversion-focused landing page, a web app, or an app interface. The primary outcome is a qualified project enquiry. The short journey is offer → clearly labeled concept work → service/process answers → enquiry → genuine storage confirmation → best-effort owner notification.

Launch scope is the portfolio home page, three fictional concept previews, services, a condensed zero-to-launch process, FAQ, enquiry form, privacy notice and accessibility statement. Post-launch scope includes a CMS, booking integrations, live app builds, detailed real client case studies and privacy-reviewed conversion measurement. Google Maps prospecting is described, not automated. No scraping, list collection, outbound email, ecommerce, accounts or dashboards are included.

## Four client-facing phases

**Discover & define.** Identify the visitor, one outcome, essential pages, constraints and approver. Confirm scope, ownership, budget and dependencies before committing to a schedule.

**Design & align.** Prepare the message and mobile-first structure before visual polish. Approve one direction and a compact reusable system. Confirm copy, rights, navigation and form states.

**Build & verify.** Complete the customer journey first, connect the real enquiry destination, validate on the server, protect secrets and check errors. Review business-critical behavior before cosmetic extras.

**Launch & hand over.** Obtain go/no-go approval, record the release and fallback, verify the actual domain and enquiry delivery, then hand over business-controlled access and maintenance guidance.

## Responsibilities and unresolved launch dependencies

The owner approves identity, business claims, contact details, privacy jurisdiction/legal text, public domain, content rights and launch timing. Manus implements the approved scope and saves version history. The owner chooses maintenance responsibility, enquiry response expectations and retention rules; these must not be represented as confirmed while missing.

The managed platform uses the same database for Preview and production. This is an explicit exception to the document's separate-staging recommendation: test application logic with mocks; avoid inserting real/test personal data silently. The separate Netlify review edition at https://akibit.netlify.app uses Netlify Forms; its publication was user-approved, but legal identity and business-launch readiness remain unconfirmed. An owner-approved real enquiry smoke test remains required before public launch.

Optional site and badge analytics are disabled in project settings; infrastructure request processing may remain. No claim of measured Core Web Vitals or fully audited WCAG compliance is made. Before launch, check a real iPhone/Safari and desktop, keyboard and zoom, and actual production receipt; target LCP ≤2.5s, INP ≤200ms and CLS ≤0.1 as goals, not claimed results.

## Go/no-go and fallback

Do not launch with privacy exposure, data loss, a broken essential action or missing required legal/business information. Cosmetic improvements may wait. Save the known-good checkpoint; first-launch fallback is to keep the previous unpublished state or serve a safe maintenance page. Rolling back code does not reverse database migrations. Keep schema changes additive.

Before broad announcement, choose the public domain, verify HTTPS and preserve existing DNS email records, review hosting costs and exports, confirm notification recipient and data access, perform an approved real enquiry test, then document launch approval. No unattended monitoring or recurring automation is created in this task.

## Akibit Studio / Arcade update

The public brand is Akibit. Studio mode uses refined Japanese-inspired paper/ink/gold styling and kintsugi-inspired ceramic artwork. Arcade mode is an optional gaming-inspired dark identity with a different hero, technical typography and panel framing. The explicit visitor choice is stored locally as akibit-theme; no tracking is added. The business goal and essential enquiry journey remain unchanged.
